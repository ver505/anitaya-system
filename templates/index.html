AIM, IMPORTANCE, HOW TO USE & CATEGORY BENCHMARKS

THE AIM OF ANITAYA

In the Philippines, smallholder farmers often make planting and loan-borrowing decisions based on intuition or historical weather patterns that are increasingly disrupted by climate variability. Crucial decisions—such as crop selection, input purchasing (fertilizers and seeds), and credit financing—are made without reliable data on expected harvest volume and financial return.

AniTaya (Ani = Harvest, Taya = Forecast/Estimate) was created to bridge data science and Filipino agriculture. Its primary aim is to empower farmers with an end-to-end, scientifically validated predictive system that forecasts crop yields in Metric Tons and standard 50-kg cavans, calculates complete production costs, and projects net farmer profit before planting begins.

WHY ANITAYA IS ESSENTIAL (IMPORTANCE)

PREVENTING DEBT & CROP LOSS By revealing production expenses and break-even selling prices per kilogram in advance, AniTaya protects farming families from entering high-interest predatory debt traps.
96.60% MACHINE LEARNING ACCURACY Powered by a Random Forest regression pipeline trained on over 25,900 international and local agricultural observations across multi-year climate cycles.
CLIMATE RESILIENCE & MONSOON ADVICE Considers PAGASA rainfall and typhoon risk to suggest when to plant and when to avoid high-risk crops during peak monsoon months.
82 PROVINCES OFFICIAL DA-AMIA GUIDE Integrates the official DA National Color-Coded Agricultural Guide (NCCAG v2.0) with BSWM dominant soil series for all Philippine provinces.

CROP SELECTION CATEGORY PRIMARY FEATURE (60.5% WEIGHT)
Each crop carries unique genetics, nutrient demand, and harvest rates. Rice yields ~3.2–4.5 MT/ha (64–90 cavans); Corn yields ~4.2–5.5 MT/ha; Potatoes yield ~16–18 MT/ha; and Cassava yields ~12–15 MT/ha. Season tags evaluate current PAGASA rainfall patterns to protect against root rotting or drought.
FARM SIZE (HECTARES) LAND AREA (HA)
Land size is measured in Hectares (HA), where 1 HA equals 10,000 square meters (~2.47 acres). Typical Filipino smallholder farms range between 0.5 HA and 3.0 HA. Total production costs and gross revenue scale directly with land area.
SELLING PRICE (₱ / KG) PSA FARMGATE BENCHMARKS
Represents the farmgate price you receive when selling harvest to traders or cooperatives. Defaults are based on PSA official benchmarks: Rice (₱22.00/kg), Corn (₱20.50/kg), Potato (₱45.00/kg), and Cassava (₱14.00/kg). Adjust this if your local buyers offer higher or lower prices.
SEASON & REGION (DA-AMIA NCCAG v2.0) PHILIPPINE AGRO-CLIMATIC
The Philippines experiences two main monsoons: Wet Season (Habagat) from June to November with heavy rains and typhoons, and Dry Season (Amihan) from December to May with high solar radiation. Our database maps all 82 provinces with dominant soil series from DA-BSWM and suitability grades (S1 Highly Suitable / S2 Moderately Suitable).
RESULTS & FINANCIAL FORMULAS NET PROFIT & CAVANS
Gross Revenue: Total kg × Selling Price.
Production Costs: Seeds + Fertilizer (N-P-K) + Agrochemicals + Fuel/Tractor + Labor. (Rice: ₱45k/ha; Corn: ₱38k/ha; Potato: ₱95k/ha; Cassava: ₱32k/ha).
Net Profit: Gross Revenue − Production Costs.
Break-Even Price: Minimum ₱/kg to avoid losses.
Sacks: Standard Philippine 50-kg cavans (kaban).
